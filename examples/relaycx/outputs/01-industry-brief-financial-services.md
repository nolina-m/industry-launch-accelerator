## Trends

### T-financial-services-1: AI in member and customer service is now mainstream, but scale depends on institution size

Going into 2026, 49% of banks and 59% of credit unions had already deployed generative AI [S4]. Adoption is uneven: enterprise-wide rollout is far more common at national banks than at midsize and community institutions [S7]. In a vendor-sponsored survey, 71% of bank and credit union leaders said they must invest in AI agents to remain competitive [S6].

- **Business impact:** Mid-market banks and credit unions feel pressure to match the service levels of national banks without their AI budgets or data teams [ASSUMPTION].
- **So what for us:** RelayCX's target sits in the adoption gap between national banks and community institutions. Lead with packaged banking and lending workflow templates rather than a build-your-own platform [ASSUMPTION].
- **Relevance:** High. Directly sizes the readiness and urgency of the mid-market buyer.
- **Evidence:** [S4] [S7] [S6]

### T-financial-services-2: Regulators oversee AI with existing rules, and chatbots that block a human are a named risk

GAO found federal financial regulators oversee AI mainly through existing laws, guidance and examinations [S8]. The CFPB has warned that chatbots that prevent access to live human support can lead to law violations and other harms [S9].

- **Business impact:** Any AI service channel must show it recognizes when a customer needs a person, and that it does not trap people in loops [ASSUMPTION].
- **So what for us:** Contextual human handoff and visible action boundaries are table stakes for this buyer, not differentiators on their own. Evidence of handoff quality (escalation triggers, context passed) is what will be examined [ASSUMPTION].
- **Relevance:** High. Shapes how compliance and risk evaluators judge any AI service agent.
- **Evidence:** [S8] [S9]

### T-financial-services-4: Generative AI fraud is raising the bar for identity verification

FinCEN reported an increase in suspicious activity reports describing deepfake media, particularly fraudulent identity documents used to get past identity verification and authentication [S12]. [VERIFY] second source needed, for example FTC impersonation fraud data.

- **Business impact:** Institutions will scrutinize any channel that can act on an account, especially text-based channels where a customer is not seen or heard [ASSUMPTION].
- **So what for us:** Identity verification before linking sensitive records is a must-show capability. RelayCX should present verification as configurable by the institution and integrated with its existing authentication, not as its own fraud control [ASSUMPTION].
- **Relevance:** High. Security evaluators will raise this first for an agent that takes actions.
- **Evidence:** [S12]

### T-financial-services-5: Vendor oversight is a gate every new AI provider must pass

Federal banking agencies expect risk management across the whole life cycle of third-party relationships [S14], and published a community bank guide to help smaller institutions apply it [S15]. [VERIFY] recent source needed on how examiners apply this to AI vendors.

- **So what for us:** Audit logs and reporting on completed, blocked and failed actions double as third-party monitoring evidence. A due diligence pack is a launch asset, not a later add-on [ASSUMPTION].

### T-financial-services-6: Consolidation keeps shrinking the number of institutions

Federally insured credit unions fell to 4,214 in Q2 2026 from 4,370 a year earlier [S13]. FDIC BankFind lists 4,219 active insured institutions [S1].

- **Business impact:** Surviving institutions are larger and are integrating acquired members, systems and processes, which adds service load [ASSUMPTION].
- **So what for us:** Merger integration is a possible trigger event: inconsistent processes and systems across merged institutions create repeat contacts that RelayCX workflows could address [ASSUMPTION].
- **Relevance:** Med. Shapes the addressable account list and buying triggers rather than the core message.
- **Evidence:** [S13] [S1]

### T-financial-services-7: Federal consumer protection is shrinking while states write their own AI rules

The CFPB has proposed sweeping staff reductions across all divisions, subject to litigation [S18]. Meanwhile Colorado enacted a law requiring notice to consumers at the point of interaction with automated decision-making technology [S19].

- **Business impact:** Compliance teams face a patchwork of state rules and uncertain federal supervision, so they favor controls that hold up under any regime [ASSUMPTION].
- **So what for us:** Do not sell on regulatory relief. Position configurable permissions, disclosures and logs as ways to meet changing rules without re-platforming [ASSUMPTION].
- **Relevance:** Med. Real but indirect; the RelayCX scope excludes lending decisions, which limits ADMT exposure.
- **Evidence:** [S18] [S19]

- **Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships, Risk Management:** Supervisory guidance on managing risk across the life cycle of third-party relationships, with a 2024 companion guide for community banks.
- **Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act privacy and safeguards rules, including the FTC Safeguards Rule:** Requires financial institutions to protect customer information. Since May 2024 the FTC rule requires non-bank financial institutions, such as consumer lenders, to report certain security breaches to the FTC.
- **TCPA consent revocation rules (FCC):** Consumers may revoke consent to calls and texts by any reasonable means, and requests must be honored within ten business days. Scheduled to take effect April 11, 2025 [VERIFY] confirm current status and any delayed provisions on fcc.gov.
- **CFPB oversight of chatbots under federal consumer financial law:** The CFPB has said deficient chatbots that block access to human support can violate the law. The bureau's capacity to supervise is shrinking under a contested restructuring.
- **Colorado SB26-189, Automated Decision-Making Technology:** Signed May 14, 2026, replacing the 2024 Colorado AI Act. It requires consumer notice at the point of interaction with covered ADMT used in consequential decisions [VERIFY] confirm the obligations start date (reported as January 1, 2027) against the enrolled text.
