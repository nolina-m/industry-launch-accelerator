### WS-1: The problem

Phones ring with simple reschedules. Patients message through the portal, then text, then call about the same appointment. Front-desk seats turn over, and cancelled slots sit empty while new patients wait weeks.

### WS-2: Why now

Practice leaders now name scheduling as their top area to automate. New patients wait about a month on average. And states like California and Texas expect patients to be told when AI is involved, with a way to reach a person.

### WS-3: How it works

Understand the request, then verify the patient using your methods. Check appointment types and open slots under your scheduling settings, confirm with the patient and book the change. Finally, send a minimal confirmation, or hand off to staff with context.

### WS-4: Value by role

Administrators: keep scheduling moving when front-desk seats sit empty. Patient access leaders: reschedules booked by your rules, with no re-keying [ASSUMPTION] (fictional capability). Privacy officers: configure AI disclosure, consent and minimal SMS content by state. Physicians: clinical questions always come to your clinical team.

### WS-5: Privacy and boundaries

RelayCX verifies patients before linking records, keeps health details out of texts, uses only provider-approved content, limits access to the actions you allow and logs everything. It never diagnoses, recommends treatment or answers clinical questions.

### WS-6: FAQ

Does it work with our EHR? Integrations are scoped per system in a pilot. Will you sign a business associate agreement? Terms are reviewed with your privacy team. Will patient data train your models? Data use is defined in the contract. Does it replace our front desk? No.
