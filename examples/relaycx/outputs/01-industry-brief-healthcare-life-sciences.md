## Trends

### T-healthcare-life-sciences-1: Front-desk and patient access roles remain the hardest seats to keep filled

Medical group turnover has stabilized overall, but pressure stays concentrated in front-desk, patient access and other roles that keep clinic flow and cash moving [S23]. Physicians also see administrative burden as the place AI can help most [S27].

- **Business impact:** Phones and inboxes back up when patient access seats are empty, which slows scheduling, intake and collections [ASSUMPTION].
- **So what for us:** RelayCX can be framed as capacity for the patient access team, finishing routine requests so staff handle exceptions, not as a replacement for staff [ASSUMPTION].
- **Relevance:** High. Speaks directly to the VP Patient Access economic buyer.
- **Evidence:** [S23] [S27]

### T-healthcare-life-sciences-2: Practices want AI at the front desk next, starting with scheduling

Asked where they want to automate with AI, practice leaders put scheduling first (31%), then calls (27%) and registration or eligibility (23%) [S22]. So far, the most common AI use has been clinical documentation through ambient listening [S24].

- **Business impact:** After documentation, front-office automation is the next AI budget line for many groups, and scheduling is the first workflow they will evaluate [ASSUMPTION].
- **So what for us:** Validates appointment rescheduling as the healthcare hero use case in the POV. Two of the three sources are MGMA, so a non-MGMA source would strengthen this [VERIFY].
- **Relevance:** High. Directly confirms buyer demand for the hero workflow.
- **Evidence:** [S22] [S24] [S27]

### T-healthcare-life-sciences-3: Patients wait about a month for a new appointment

The average new-patient appointment wait across 15 large metro areas was 31 days in 2025, up 19% from 2022 [S25]. Waits were longest in specialties such as obstetrics and gynecology [S26]. [VERIFY] both sources report the same AMN survey; an independent second source is needed.

- **Business impact:** Every unfilled cancellation and no-show is lost access for patients waiting weeks, and lost revenue for the practice [ASSUMPTION].
- **So what for us:** Fast, verified rescheduling can refill freed slots. Any claim about reduced wait times or recovered slots needs pilot data [PROOF NEEDED].
- **Relevance:** Med. Strong context for specialty practices, though RelayCX does not add capacity itself.
- **Evidence:** [S25] [S26]

### T-healthcare-life-sciences-4: Patients prefer text for reminders, but self-service scheduling lags

In a large patient survey, 48% preferred text reminders, ahead of phone calls and email [S28]. [VERIFY] the survey date isn't shown, and a recent, independent source is needed before this is used in messaging.

- **Business impact:** SMS is an accepted channel for patients, which makes it a natural place to complete simple follow-ups [ASSUMPTION].
- **So what for us:** Supports the SMS leg of the RelayCX story. Keep SMS content minimal and link to secure flows for anything sensitive, as the brief proposes [ASSUMPTION].
- **Relevance:** Med. Relevant to the channel strategy, but the evidence is thin and vendor-sourced.

### T-healthcare-life-sciences-5: States now require providers to disclose AI in patient communications

California requires disclaimers on generative AI patient communications about clinical information, with instructions for reaching a human [S29]. Texas requires providers to disclose AI used in a patient's care, effective January 1, 2026 [S30], no later than the date of service [S31].

- **Business impact:** Multistate practices must configure AI disclosures by state and channel, and show patients a path to a human [ASSUMPTION].
- **So what for us:** Configurable disclosures and human handoff are must-haves. Keeping RelayCX to administrative tasks and routing clinical questions to staff limits exposure, but buyers will still ask how disclosures work [ASSUMPTION].
- **Relevance:** High. A direct requirement for how RelayCX conversations must be configured.
- **Evidence:** [S29] [S30] [S31]

### T-healthcare-life-sciences-6: Vendor breaches and HIPAA enforcement are putting business associates under scrutiny

The largest healthcare data breach reported in 2025 occurred at a business associate [S35]. OCR announced 16 resolution agreements in January to August 2025 over failures to complete an accurate risk analysis [S34]. An overhaul of the HIPAA Security Rule remains pending [S33].

- **Business impact:** Practices will expect a business associate agreement, a security review and evidence of risk analysis before connecting any vendor to scheduling or billing systems [ASSUMPTION].
- **So what for us:** Least-privilege permissions, action allowlists and access logs are central to the healthcare story. No certifications can be claimed today, so the security narrative must rest on controls and documentation [VERIFY].
- **Relevance:** High. Security and privacy evaluators can block a deal.
- **Evidence:** [S35] [S34] [S33]

- **California AB 3030, generative AI in health care services:** Effective January 1, 2025. Providers using generative AI for patient communications about clinical information must include a disclaimer and instructions for reaching a human, unless a licensed provider reviews the message [VERIFY] confirm against the bill text.
- **Texas HB 149 (Texas Responsible AI Governance Act), health care disclosure:** Effective January 1, 2026. Providers using AI in relation to a patient's health care service or treatment must disclose it, no later than the date of service [VERIFY] confirm scope against the enrolled text.
- **TCPA health care messaging exemption and consent revocation rules:** Certain health care messages, such as appointment reminders, from HIPAA covered entities and business associates get a limited TCPA exemption. Billing and other financial messages are not covered. The 2025 consent revocation rules also apply.
- **Colorado SB26-189, Automated Decision-Making Technology:** Requires consumer notice at the point of interaction with automated decision-making technology used in consequential decisions, which can include health care services [VERIFY] confirm scope and start date.
