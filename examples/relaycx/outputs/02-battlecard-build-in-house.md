## Overview

The institution builds its own AI service agent on a general-purpose model. Many institutions are already deploying generative AI [S4], but data readiness is a known weakness for community institutions [S5].

## Strengths and weaknesses

| Strengths | Weaknesses |

| Full control over data, workflows and model choice [ASSUMPTION]. | Data and governance gaps can undermine AI projects at community institutions [S5]. |

## Where we win

- Faster time to a governed, auditable workflow than building controls, logs and handoffs from scratch [ASSUMPTION].

## Where we lose

- Institutions with strong in-house engineering and a mandate to build [ASSUMPTION].

## Landmine questions to plant in discovery

- Who will maintain the integrations, permissions and audit logs after launch?
- How will model risk management validate a home-built agent that takes actions?

## Traps to avoid

- Comparing build cost to license cost without counting ongoing maintenance and validation [ASSUMPTION].

## Top objections when they're in the deal

### "We can build this on an LLM API ourselves."

- **Underlying concern:** Cost and control.
- **Usually from:** CIO, IT
- **Acknowledge:** Building is a real option, especially with generative AI already in use [S4].
- **Reframe:** The model is the easy part; identity checks, permissions, approvals, handoffs and logs are most of the work.
- **Evidence:** Cornerstone warns that unclean data amplifies dysfunction in AI projects [S5].
- **Ask back:** How long would it take your team to build and validate the controls, not just the chatbot?
